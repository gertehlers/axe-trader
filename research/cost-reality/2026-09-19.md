# Cost reality check — where must an edge be biggest to survive?

Spec §6.1 step 1, run 2026-09-19. Source: `research/experiments/2026-09-19-cost-reality-check.py`
and `.json`. This measures the market, not a strategy, so nothing here can be overfitted and the
2026-08-01 holdout does not apply — the full history is used.

## Why this was run before the next hypothesis

Three hypotheses have died and none of them died on signal:

- [[H-0001-rsi-bb-mean-reversion]] — `rejected: no signal`.
- [[H-0002-short-horizon-continuation]] — real direction, `uneconomic`: +0.565 pts/hour against a
  0.561-pt spread.
- [[H-0003-continuation-longer-horizon]] — the edge-to-cost ratio ceilings at ~1.55 spreads and
  stops improving with holding time.

All three were run on **US500 5-minute bars**, and that choice was never justified — it is simply
what the archived Java engine used. Before spending another hypothesis, it is worth knowing how
expensive that ground is compared with the alternatives we hold data for.

## What is measured

Per instrument × timeframe, in price points:

    spread      mean(close_ask − close_bid) — the round trip, paid once
    financing   P(the bar spans the 21:00 UTC charge cut-off) × price × |worst daily rate| / 100
    hurdle      spread + financing
    median TR   median true range of one bar — the move actually on offer
    cost/TR     hurdle ÷ median TR  ← the headline

`cost/TR` is the fraction of a typical bar's range consumed by trading it once. A strategy at that
timeframe must capture more than this share of a typical bar before it earns anything at all.

## What it says

**1. Every hypothesis so far has been tested on the 11th-cheapest of 15 available grounds.** US500
5m costs 20.7% of a typical bar's range per round trip. US500 4h costs 3.5% — the same instrument,
the same data, **six times cheaper** relative to the move on offer. That is the single largest
free improvement available, and it required no new idea, only pointing an existing one somewhere
else.

**2. Spread is near-constant in points, so the timeframe choice is almost entirely what sets the
hurdle.** US500's spread is 0.559 at 5m and 0.548 at 4h — essentially identical. The median true
range goes from 2.70 to 21.40. Nothing about the cost improves when you slow down; the *move*
grows while the cost does not.

**3. Financing only becomes visible at 4h and above, and on US500 it is not negligible.** US500 1d
carries 1.30 pts of expected financing against a 0.69-pt spread — financing is **two-thirds of the
daily hurdle**, because index long rates (−0.0215%/day) are large relative to a 0.69-pt spread on a
~6,000-point instrument. It still only totals 3.0% of a daily range, but any daily US500 hypothesis
must be net-of-financing from the first run, and a long-only one is paying that rate every night.

**4. Oil is the cheapest instrument in relative terms and US500 is the cheapest in the round.**
OIL_CRUDE 1d at 2.4% is the single best ratio on the board, and OIL_CRUDE beats OIL_BRENT at every
timeframe (0.0351 vs 0.0414 spread on near-identical ranges) — if a hypothesis works on one it
should be tested on the cheaper one first.

**5. NATURALGAS has been excluded from research entirely** (owner, 2026-09-19), so it does not
appear above. The earlier run included it and it placed 8th, 12th, 16th and 19th of 19 — never the
cheapest ground at any timeframe — on top of a 16.2% missing-core defect Capital.com cannot supply.
Do not add it back; see `research/EXCLUDED-INSTRUMENTS.md`.

## Consequence for the next hypothesis

Aim at **4h and daily, on US500 and OIL_CRUDE**. 5-minute bars on anything are a bad hunting ground
and 5-minute bars on oil are the worst on the board. This does not resurrect the continuation
family — [[H-0003-continuation-longer-horizon]] closed it on its own evidence, and its ceiling was
measured in spreads, which this does not change. It changes where the *next* idea is pointed.

## Caveats

- **Slippage is not in the hurdle**, because there is no data source for it (open question, carried
  from the 2026-09-18 handover). Every number here is therefore a floor on the true cost, and the
  floor is tighter at short timeframes where fills are more likely to slip relative to a small
  range.
- **The weekend financing multiplier defaults to 1.0**, which understates any position carried over
  a weekend. This matters most for the daily rows, which are the top of the ranking.
- **Financing is an expected cost per one-bar hold**, computed from the share of bars whose window
  spans 21:00 UTC. A real strategy's financing depends on its actual holding pattern.
