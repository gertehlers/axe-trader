# Which ground next? — the directional half of the question

Source: `research/experiments/2026-09-19-ground-selection.py` and `.json`, run 2026-09-19.
Companion to `research/cost-reality/2026-09-19.md`, which answered the cost half.

This measures the market, not a strategy, so nothing here can be overfitted and the 2026-08-01
holdout does not apply — the full history is used, exactly as the cost check did.

## Why this was run

The cost check ranked 15 grounds by how much of a typical bar's range a round trip eats, and said
plainly that every hypothesis so far had been run on expensive ground. That is necessary and not
sufficient: **cheap ground with no directional structure is still untradeable.**

And by 2026-09-19 three independent routes had said direction was unavailable on OIL_CRUDE 15m:

- [[H-0006]] — median favourable excursion +1.67 ATR against adverse +2.08 ATR.
- [[H-0007]] — a filter that doubles the odds of a fast run, but the side is a coin flip
  (47.9% / 50.8% LONG).
- [[H-0008]] — and a breakout cannot convert that timing, because the break carries no directional
  information either (−0.0851R, placebo percentile 74.5).

So before choosing the next ground, measure whether *any* of them carries directional structure.

## What is measured

Lo-MacKinlay variance ratio per instrument × timeframe, at lags 2, 4, 8, 16:

    VR(q) = Var(q-bar return) / (q × Var(1-bar return))

1.0 is a random walk — nothing to extract. Above 1 the ground trends (momentum is available);
below 1 it mean-reverts. `z*` is the heteroskedasticity-robust statistic; **|z| > 2 is a real
deviation, and any VR without it is a number to ignore however far from 1 it sits.**

Log returns on mid prices. Returns spanning a session gap are dropped — a move across an overnight
close is not a q-bar move.

## What it says

**1. No ground shows directional structure. 60 cells tested, one significant, three expected by
chance.** The single |z| > 2 (US500 daily, lag 2) is *fewer* hits than noise alone would produce at
α = 0.05. It is not evidence and must not be treated as a lead.

**2. Nothing trends anywhere.** 53 of 60 cells sit below 1.0 (mean VR 0.931). Not one ground shows
VR meaningfully above 1 at any lag. **Momentum is not available on any instrument or timeframe we
hold** — which is the common ancestor of H-0002, H-0003, H-0007 and H-0008, all of which were
continuation designs in one form or another.

**3. The sub-1 tilt at fast timeframes is probably microstructure, not opportunity.** Bid-ask bounce
induces negative autocorrelation in returns and biases VR below 1 by construction. On ground where
the spread is 20–45% of a bar's range, that is the leading explanation for VR ≈ 0.96–0.99 — and it
lives *inside* the spread, so it cannot be collected. This also means the intraday VR numbers should
not be read as mean reversion worth trading.

**4. The one consistent qualitative pattern is mean reversion strengthening with horizon, on
daily.** VR(16) is 0.641 / 0.709 / 0.759 across the three instruments — the same sign, the same
shape, on the cheapest ground (2.4–4.0% cost/TR) where microstructure is least able to explain it.
**And it is exactly where there is no power**: 843–848 bars, about 3.3 years. Individually not one
of them is significant, and the three are not independent (Brent and WTI are the same underlying).

## The recommendation

**Do not point another directional hypothesis at any of these 15 grounds.** There is no measured
structure to aim at, and running one anyway is how the previous eight hypotheses were spent.

The binding constraint has changed. It is no longer ideas — it is **data**. The only pattern worth
pursuing sits on daily bars, where we hold 3.3 years and would need decades to resolve it. This is
the identical wall [[H-0004]] hit, whose parked condition was recorded as *"31 years of data, not a
better idea."*

So the highest-value next action is **acquisition, not analysis**: obtain long daily history
(20–30 years) for US500, OIL_CRUDE and OIL_BRENT. Daily history that far back is cheap and widely
available, unlike intraday, and it is the one thing that would convert the mean-reversion-at-horizon
pattern from a shape into a testable hypothesis with real power.

## What this does not prove

**A variance ratio tests *unconditional linear* structure.** A market can be a random walk overall
and still carry structure conditional on something — a volatility state, a session, an event. This
measurement cannot rule that out, and it would be wrong to read it as "these markets are
unbeatable."

What it does say is that any such structure must be **conditional**, which raises the evidential
bar rather than lowering it: a conditional effect needs its own pre-registration and a placebo that
matches on the conditioning variable. And three conditional approaches have now been tried on
OIL_CRUDE 15m (H-0006, H-0007, H-0008), all of which failed — consistent with what this table says
about that ground.
